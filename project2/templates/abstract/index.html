    Project 2 — Research Abstract¶

    Author(s): one or two names · Date: · Working title:

    Due Fri, Oct 2. 150–250 words, written as one or two paragraphs of prose — not headings, not bullets. This is your paper's abstract, written before the research instead of after it. You will revise it; the point is that the logic has to hold together now.

    Four moves, in this order. Most published abstracts in software engineering make exactly these four.

    1 · Context — what is going on, and why a reader should care¶

    One or two sentences. Where does your work sit? What is the reader assumed to know? Write for a classmate in this course, not for your instructor.

    "Developers increasingly ask language models to write unit tests, and tools that do so now ship in mainstream IDEs."

    2 · The problem — what the literature leaves open¶

    Two or three sentences, and name the papers. This is the move students skip, and it is the one that decides whether you have a project. A gap is not "nobody has done exactly my thing"; it is a question the existing work raises and does not answer.

    "Existing evaluations (Schäfer et al. 2024; Lemieux et al. 2023) report coverage, which measures what tests execute rather than what they would catch. Whether the source a model is given — a specification or the implementation itself — changes the tests' fault-detection power has not been measured."

    3 · The proposed solution — what you will actually do¶

    Two or three sentences: the study or the system, the data, the comparison. Concrete enough that a reader can picture the experiment.

    "We generate two test suites per function from the same model — one from the docstring alone, one from the body alone — over N documented Python functions, and compare their mutation scores against injected faults."

    4 · What you expect to find — a claim that could be false¶

    One or two sentences. State the expected result and say what the opposite result would mean; both must be worth writing up.

    "We expect specification-derived suites to detect more injected faults. If they do not, the widely repeated advice to 'generate tests from the spec' is not supported at this scale — which is equally worth reporting."

    Before you submit¶

    • [ ] Solo or pair stated at the top (and with whom)
    • [ ] 150–250 words, prose, no headings
    • [ ] At least two real papers named in move 2 — ones you actually opened
    • [ ] Move 4 states something that could turn out to be false
    • [ ] A reader who knows nothing about your topic can follow it

    Being wrong later is fine and expected — projects change. Being vague now is the failure this exercise exists to catch.
