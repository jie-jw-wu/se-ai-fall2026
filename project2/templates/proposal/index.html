    Project 2 Proposal¶

    Author(s): one or two names · Date: · Working title:

    Two to three pages. Prose, not bullet fragments — if you cannot write it as prose you do not yet have a project.

    Write it to be lifted. Sections 1–5 of this proposal are your paper's introduction and method section, version zero. On this timeline (about twenty working days of execution), a team that writes the proposal well has two pages of the paper done on Oct 30; a team that treats it as paperwork writes those pages twice.

    1. The claim (one sentence)¶

    State what you believe, in a form that could turn out to be false.

    Weak: "We will investigate LLM test generation." Strong: "Tests generated from a specification catch more injected faults than tests generated from the implementation, on the same functions."

    2. Why it matters¶

    Who changes what they do if you are right? Two or three sentences.

    3. What is already known¶

    Four to eight papers, and what each one leaves open. This section is where supervisors find out whether the project is real. If everything you cite says your question is answered, you need a different question.

    4. Method¶

    • What you will build or run
    • The dataset or benchmark, and why that one
    • The baseline — and be honest about whether you might lose to it
    • Independent and dependent variables; what is held fixed
    • How many runs, and how you will report variance

    5. How you will know¶

    • The metric, and why it measures the claim rather than a convenient proxy
    • What result would make you wrong. If nothing would, this is not yet research
    • How contamination is handled, if a public benchmark is involved

    6. Feasibility¶

    • Compute you need and where it comes from
    • The week-by-week plan to Week 14
    • The riskiest assumption, and what you do if it fails. Every proposal has one; naming it is not weakness

    7. Threats you can already see¶

    You will not have solved these. Name them anyway.

    8. Team¶

    Who does what. Two to three people, and everyone contributes to the writing.
