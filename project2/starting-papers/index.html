    Starting Papers¶

    Where to begin reading for your radar and your project. You do not need to read all of this — the radar asks for three papers actually opened, and this page is where to find three good ones.

    The course's complete bibliography (100+ papers and books, everything the slides cite) is on the References page; every slide that makes a specific claim cites its source at the foot.

    The frontier, mapped¶

    Start here to see where the field is going:

    • Hassan et al., Agentic Software Engineering: Foundational Pillars and a Research Roadmap (arXiv:2509.06216) — the research agenda this course's ⚙ thread follows; nearly every section ends in an open question sized for a Project 2.
    • Hassan et al., Towards AI-Native Software Engineering (SE 3.0) (arXiv:2410.06107) — the shorter vision paper.
    • AI Teammates in SE 3.0 — a curated, living paper collection from the SAIL group: https://github.com/SAILResearch/AI_Teammates_in_SE3. Skim the list; chase what catches you.
    • ICSE 2027 Competition Track — https://conf.researchr.org/track/icse-2027/icse-2027-competition-track — a live view of what the community considers worth competing on; also a possible extra venue for a strong artifact.

    Code LLMs and their evaluation¶

    • Chen et al., Evaluating Large Language Models Trained on Code (2021) — Codex and pass@k; the paper behind half of Module 1's evaluation lecture.
    • Li et al., StarCoder (2023) — what an open code-LLM release looks like.
    • Liu et al., Is Your Code Generated by ChatGPT Really Correct? (2023) — EvalPlus; why weak tests inflate benchmark scores.

    Agents for SE¶

    • Jimenez et al., SWE-bench (2024) — how agent progress is scored.
    • Yang et al., SWE-agent (2024) — the agent-computer interface argument.
    • Yao et al., ReAct (2023) — the reason-then-act loop underneath it all.
    • Wang et al., OpenHands (2024) — an open agent platform to experiment on.

    AI-assisted development — the human evidence¶

    • Peng et al., The Impact of AI on Developer Productivity (2023) — the controlled Copilot experiment.
    • Perry et al., Do Users Write More Insecure Code with AI Assistants? (2022) — the security half of the same question.
    • Pearce et al., Asleep at the Keyboard? (IEEE S&P 2022) — CWE-level security of generated code; topic B5 replicates its shape.

    Doing empirical SE research well¶

    • Ralph et al., ACM SIGSOFT Empirical Standards for Software Engineering Research — what reviewers will check your paper against; read the standard matching your study type before writing the proposal.

    How to find more¶

    1. Chase citations — take one paper above and read what it cites and what cites it (Google Scholar's "cited by").
    2. Skim recent proceedings — ICSE, FSE, ASE, MSR, EASE, AIware; the last two years are enough.
    3. Mine the lectures — every deck's closing slides name open questions, and every claim's source is at the slide's foot.
